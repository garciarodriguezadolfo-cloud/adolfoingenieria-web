When should you commission a preliminary technical report?

When to request a preliminary technical report after an accident involving machinery or an installation, what it can clarify and when further investigation is needed.

16 November 2026 · Author: Manuel Adolfo García Rodríguez
Mechanical Engineer | Technical Expert

Introduction

After an accident involving a machine or an installation, the cause may be unclear. A preliminary technical report sets out hypotheses about what happened and identifies how to test them. It is particularly useful when individuals or businesses have suffered harm and a compensation claim may be considered.

When to request one

As soon as possible if the equipment is due to be repaired or dismantled. Before its condition changes, it is advisable to document it and gather the original photographs, the manual and maintenance records.

What it can clarify

A broken component may have caused the accident or been damaged during it. Examining the equipment, the installation and the available data helps assess which hypotheses are consistent with the evidence and what still needs to be verified.

When to extend the investigation

If uncertainty about the cause is relevant to the claim, the preliminary report helps determine whether to commission a full expert assessment and which measurements, tests or documents will be needed.

Technical references

  • INSST (2001). NTP 592. Tratamiento documental e investigación de accidentes.
  • Health and Safety Executive (2004). HSG245. Investigating accidents and incidents.
